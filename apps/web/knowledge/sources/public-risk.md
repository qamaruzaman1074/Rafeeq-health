### risk.carrier.heading

What does being a carrier mean?

### risk.carrier.body

A carrier has a genetic variant associated with a particular inherited condition. In many recessive conditions, carriers do not have the condition themselves. However, if both biological parents carry relevant disease-associated variants in the same gene, there may be a chance that a child inherits the condition.

### risk.carrier.note

Being a carrier does not automatically mean that a person is ill or that their future children will have an inherited condition.

### risk.couple.heading

Why one result is not enough to determine a couple's risk.

### risk.couple.body

An individual screening result provides information about that individual. Estimating potential risk for a child generally requires consideration of both biological parents' relevant findings, the specific gene and variants involved, the inheritance pattern, and clinical interpretation.

### risk.family.heading

Understanding family relationships and inherited risk without judgement.

### risk.family.body

Biological relatives may have a higher chance of sharing certain inherited variants because they have common ancestors. However, a family relationship alone does not establish that a child will inherit a particular condition.

Individual and couple-level screening information, family history, and appropriate professional interpretation are important.

### risk.diagnosis.heading

Risk is not diagnosis

### risk.diagnosis.body

A calculated or estimated risk is not the same as a diagnosis. Screening can identify findings that may need further assessment, but it cannot always establish whether a person or future child has a condition.

### risk.next.heading

After screening: confirmatory assessment and options

### risk.next.items

Screening and diagnosis are different. Some findings need confirmatory assessment before any conclusion is drawn.

Ambiguous or inconclusive results should be interpreted by a qualified professional.

Genetic counselling can help you understand findings, uncertainty and available options.

Options may include further testing, specialist consultation and reproductive-genomics services where available. Which options are appropriate depends on individual circumstances, clinical context, availability and personal values.
