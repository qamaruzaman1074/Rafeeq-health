## What does it mean to be a carrier?

A carrier has one changed gene copy. The test shows what was found; it is not a diagnosis.

_Source block: learn.carrier.short_

## Is carrier screening a diagnosis?

Screening is not a diagnosis. Clinicians confirm what a result means for health.

_Source block: learn.risk-vs-diagnosis.short_

## If we both carry the same change, what is the chance for each pregnancy?

For autosomal recessive conditions, when both partners carry a pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant in the same gene, each pregnancy has about a 1 in 4 chance of an affected child, a 1 in 2 chance of a carrier child, and a 1 in 4 chance of a child who inherits neither familial variant. Each pregnancy is independent.

_Source block: result.overlap.formula_

## What if my result is uncertain?

Some results stay uncertain until more review. A person can help when numbers do not apply.

_Source block: learn.uncertainty.short_

## Who can see my overlap result?

When both partners carry a change in the same gene, each pregnancy can be looked at separately. The numbers describe chances, not certainties.

_Source block: glossary.couple-result_

## What usually happens after a screening result?

Next steps often include counselling and follow-up with your clinician.
